Mapelago

All Mapelago games

Mapelago has eight daily geography games. They're all free, with no account and nothing to install, and everyone in the world gets the same puzzles each day. The two Bigger games take about a minute. Mystery city takes about three. Start with Mystery city, the tile-and-pin game where Mapelago began, then pick whatever catches your eye. Our turtle won't judge a wrong pin.

Mystery city

Mystery city is Mapelago's daily guess-the-city game. You start with 1,000 points and a board of number tiles. Slide them to merge matching pairs, like 2048. Each new tile size you make, up to 64, earns a clue: hemisphere, continent, climate and coast, city size and language, its country's shape, then the city's name. Every move costs 6 points, so each clue is a small bet, and each new tile size adds half its value back: a 16 adds 8. When you think you know it, drop a pin. You get three, and a pin within 50 km finds the city. A miss costs 50 to 150 points, less the closer it lands. It tells you how far off you were, which way to go, whether you're getting warmer, and opens the next clue for free. Stuck on a city you don't know? Ask the turtle where in its country it is, for 80 points. Short on time? Pick Quick mode before your first move: no tiles, one clue to start, a new clue after each missed pin, and five pins. Your result shows the sum and up to three stars. Everyone plays the same city, and a new one arrives at midnight, your time.

  1. Slide the tiles. Two matching tiles merge into one worth double.
  2. Each new tile size, up to 64, unlocks a clue and adds half its value. Every move costs 6 of your 1,000 points.
  3. When you have a guess, drop a pin on the map. You get three.
  4. A pin within 50 km finds the city. A miss costs 50 to 150 points, less the closer it lands, and shows the distance, an arrow toward it, whether you're warmer or colder, and opens the next clue.
  5. Or pick Quick mode: one clue to start, one more after each missed pin, five pins. Find it on pin 1 for 1,000 points, pin 2 for 800, and so on.
  6. Share your result: your stars, your clues and how close each pin came, never the city.

Mystery country

Mystery country is the guess-the-country version of Mapelago, and it plays by the same rules as Mystery city. Slide the tiles to merge matching pairs, like 2048. Each new tile size you make, up to 64, earns a clue, in this order: hemisphere, continent, coast and borders, languages, the first letter of the capital, then the country's outline. Every move costs 6 of your 1,000 points, so each clue is a small bet, and each new tile size adds half its value back: a 16 adds 8. When you think you know it, drop a pin. You get three. A pin inside the country, or within 50 km of it, counts as found. A miss costs 50 to 150 points, less the closer it lands. It tells you how far your pin was from the nearest point of the country, which way to go and whether you're getting warmer, and it opens the next clue for free. Your result shows the sum and up to three stars. Short on time? Pick Quick mode before your first move: no tiles, one clue to start, a new clue after each missed pin, and five pins. Everyone gets the same country, and a new one arrives at midnight, your time.

  1. Slide the tiles. Two matching tiles merge into one worth double.
  2. Each new tile size, up to 64, unlocks a clue and adds half its value, ending with the country's outline. Every move costs 6 of your 1,000 points.
  3. When you have a guess, drop a pin on the map. You get three.
  4. A pin inside the country, or within 50 km of it, finds it. A miss costs 50 to 150 points, less the closer it lands, and shows the distance to the nearest point of the country, an arrow toward it, whether you're warmer or colder, and opens the next clue.
  5. Or pick Quick mode: one clue to start, one more after each missed pin, five pins. Find it on pin 1 for 1,000 points, pin 2 for 800, and so on.
  6. Share your result: your stars, your clues and how close each pin came, never the country.

Bigger city?

Bigger city? is Mapelago's one-minute population game. You see two cities and tap the one where more people live. Each reveal shows both populations, so a few answers may surprise you. There are ten pairs a day. They start far apart and get closer, so the last few are the hard ones. Everyone in the world gets the same ten pairs, and your result is a row of ten ticks and crosses. New pairs arrive at midnight, your time.

  1. Look at the two cities.
  2. Tap the one where more people live.
  3. Go through all ten pairs. They get closer as you go.
  4. See your result: a row of ten ticks and crosses to share.

Bigger country?

Bigger country? is Mapelago's one-minute size game. You see two countries and tap the one that covers more land. Sizes are total areas, using official figures where they differ from the map outlines, and countries whose size depends on what is counted are left out. Each reveal shows both countries at the same scale. There are ten pairs a day. They start far apart and get closer, so expect a close call or two at the end. Everyone in the world gets the same ten pairs, so you can compare notes with a friend. New pairs arrive at midnight, your time.

  1. Look at the two countries.
  2. Tap the one that covers more land.
  3. Go through all ten pairs. The gaps shrink as you go.

Voyage

Voyage is Mapelago's daily trivia trip. There are ten islands, each with one geography question about capitals, country outlines, borders, a spot on the globe, sizes or languages. Each island is worth more than the last, up to 1,000 points. After every right answer you choose: drop anchor and keep your points, or sail on. A wrong answer ends the trip and leaves you with the points from your last harbor, at islands 3 and 6. You get one spyglass a day, which takes away two wrong answers. Everyone gets the same ten questions, and a new voyage sets sail at midnight, your time.

  1. Answer the island's question. Your one spyglass a day removes two wrong answers.
  2. After a right answer, choose: drop anchor to keep your points, or sail on.
  3. Each island is worth more than the last, up to 1,000 points.
  4. A wrong answer ends the trip. You keep the points from your last harbor, at island 3 or 6.

Fathoms

Fathoms is Mapelago's daily naming game. You get seven prompts, such as a country that borders Germany or a capital city in Africa. Each prompt has many right answers, and you give one. The less obvious your answer, the deeper it goes, up to 1,000 fathoms a prompt and 7,000 a day. Depth follows a fixed rule: among the right answers, the country whose largest city is smallest goes deepest. After each prompt you see every right answer, deepest first. A wrong answer scores nothing, and you can pass on a prompt. Everyone gets the same seven prompts, and new ones arrive at midnight, your time.

  1. Read the prompt, such as a country that borders Germany.
  2. Type one country that fits. Skip the first one that comes to mind if you can.
  3. Score up to 1,000 fathoms. The country whose largest city is smallest goes deepest.
  4. A wrong answer scores nothing. Pass if you're stuck.
  5. Play all seven prompts, for up to 7,000 fathoms.

Stamps

Stamps is Mapelago's daily trivia wheel. Spin it and it lands on one of six categories: capitals, borders, languages, size, continents or coasts. Answer the geography question it gives you, and a right answer stamps that category. Miss, and the category gets one more chance with a new question. Miss again and it stays empty. The goal is to fill all six in as few spins as you can. Everyone gets the same questions each day, and the page clears at midnight, your time.

  1. Spin the wheel. Tap it to stop the spin early.
  2. Answer the question in the category it lands on.
  3. Get it right and that category gets its stamp.
  4. Get it wrong and you get one more try with a new question. Miss twice and it stays empty.
  5. Fill all six in as few spins as you can.

Crossings

Crossings is Mapelago's daily route game. You get two countries joined by land, such as France and Poland, and you name the countries in between until your chain links them border to border. Par is the shortest route. Each country you name is coloured by how far it is from a shortest route, so the map warms up as you close in. Every guess beyond par costs 100 points, and a hint, which reveals a country on a shortest route, costs 150. Six guesses beyond par and the border stays shut. Only land borders count, so island countries can't be on a route. Borders that exist only through an exclave or overseas territory, such as Kaliningrad or French Guiana, aren't used in daily crossings. Monday's crossing is short and Sunday's is a long haul. Everyone gets the same crossing, and a new one arrives at midnight, your time.

  1. Read today's two countries and par, the fewest borders between them.
  2. Type a country you'd pass through on the way.
  3. Its colour shows how far it is from a shortest route. The darkest colour is on one.
  4. Keep naming countries until your chain joins the two, border to border.
  5. Score 1,000 at par. Each extra guess costs 100 and each hint 150.

Streaks

Each game keeps its own streak. Every 7 days you play a game earns a streak freeze for it (you can hold two); a freeze covers a day you miss. "Your streaks", at the foot of every game, shows them all and gives you a save code to move them to another device.

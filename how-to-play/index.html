Mapelago

How to play Mapelago

Mapelago is a daily puzzle with one mystery city. You earn clues by sliding tiles on a small board, then you pin the city on a world map. A game takes about three minutes. Rather guess a country? Mystery country plays by the same rules.

The four steps

  1. Slide the tiles. Swipe on a phone, or use the arrow keys or the on-screen arrows. Two tiles with the same number join into one tile worth double.
  2. Earn clues. The first time you make a new tile size, the game gives you a clue about the city.
  3. Drop a pin. Open the map whenever you like and tap where you think the city is. You get three pins.
  4. Read the miss. A pin that misses tells you how far off it was, which way to go, and whether you are already in the right country. It also opens the next clue for free.

The tiles change color as they grow, from open water to land, like the depths on a sea chart.

Scoring

You start with 1,000 points. Every move costs 6 points and every missed pin costs 150. Put a pin within 50 km of the city and you've found it. Your score is the points you have left.

From No. 14 (27 November 2026), in both Mystery city and Mystery country, each new tile size, up to 64, adds half its value back the first time you make it: a 4 adds 2, a 16 adds 8, a 64 adds 32. A missed pin costs 50 points if it lands within 250 km of the answer, rising evenly to 150 at 2,000 km or more. A find with no moves is still the only way to score 1,000: a tile never pays back the moves it took to make.

The end card shows the sum, for example "1,000 − 8 moves × 6 + 14 tile bonus − 1 missed pin (50) = 916", and one to three stars: three at 850 or more, two at 650, one at 400. Quick mode keeps its own scoring (1,000 on the first pin, 800 on the second, and so on) and gets the stars too. A round you started before the change keeps the scoring it began with.

If all three pins miss, you keep part of what you would have had for finding it with your third pin. The closer your best pin was, the more you keep: about 83% for a pin 120 km away, 57% at 500 km, 38% at 1,000 km, and nothing from 2,500 km.

Stuck on a city you don't know? Once all the clues up to the country's shape are open, you can ask the turtle where in the country the city is: north or south, east or west, near a border or not. It costs 80 points.

So the puzzle is a trade. More moves buy more clues, and every clue costs points. A careful player spends about 300 points to earn all six. Once all six are open, more moves only cost points, so drop your pin.

Mystery country

From No. 14, on 27 November 2026, Mystery country plays by the rules below. A round started before then keeps the rules it began with: moves cost 8 points and a miss doesn't open a clue.

Mystery country plays by the same rules as Mystery city, with a country as the answer. Every move costs 6 points, and each new tile size adds half its value back. A missed pin costs 50 to 150 points, less the closer it lands, and opens the next clue for free. If all three pins miss, you keep part of your score in the same way, by how close your best pin came.

The six clues are the country's hemisphere, continent, coast and borders, languages, the first letter of its capital and, last, its outline. Put a pin inside the country, or within 50 km of it, and you've found it. A miss tells you how far your pin was from the nearest point of the country (its nearest border or coast), which way to go, and whether you're getting warmer. There's no turtle to ask: the outline is the last clue.

From No. 14 you can also play Mystery country in Quick mode, as in Mystery city: pick it before your first move. There are no tiles. You get one clue to start, a new clue after each missed pin, and five pins. A find on the first pin scores 1,000, on the second 800, and so on down to 200; five misses score up to 100 by how close your best pin came.

Voyage, the trivia trip

Voyage is Mapelago's daily trivia trip. There are ten islands, each with one geography question: capitals, outlines, borders, a spot on the globe, which city has the most people, languages, money, highest peaks, phone codes, web addresses, which side of the road people drive on, or landlocked countries. Each island is worth more than the last, up to 1,000 points. After every right answer you choose: drop anchor and keep your points, or sail on. A wrong answer ends the trip and leaves you with the points from your last harbor, at islands 3 and 6. You get one spyglass a day, which takes away two wrong answers. Everyone gets the same ten questions, and a new voyage sets sail at midnight, your time. When your trip is over, Play more gives you endless practice questions that never change your score or streak.

Fathoms, the naming game

Fathoms is Mapelago's daily naming game. You get seven prompts, such as a country that borders Germany or a capital city in Africa. Each prompt has many right answers, and you give one. The less obvious your answer, the deeper it goes, up to 1,000 fathoms a prompt and 7,000 a day. Depth follows a fixed rule: among the right answers, the country whose largest city is smallest goes deepest. After each prompt you see every right answer, deepest first. A wrong answer scores nothing, and you can pass on a prompt. Everyone gets the same seven prompts, and new ones arrive at midnight, your time.

The world in earlier years

On the map where you drop your pin, the menu in the corner redraws the world as it stood in 1914, 1950 or 1980. Countries that shared a ruler, such as the lands of the Soviet Union or the British Empire, lose the borders between them. It changes nothing about the answer or the score. After the game, the result has a link that lists who ruled the mystery place's land in each of those years, and another that opens the old map so you can tap any spot and see who held it. These maps are drawn on today's borders, so they are a simplification.

Logbook, the history game

Logbook is Mapelago's daily history game. Each day brings eight questions about how the map has changed since 1800: which of two events came first, when something happened, what a country used to be called, who it became independent from, and where its capital moved. Some questions put a year on a timeline: slide to your guess and the game tells you how hot or cold you are, from a bullseye to freezing. Land within 5 years and it counts. The questions get harder through the round, and through the week, from easy on Monday to hardest on Sunday. Every fact comes from a source-checked list of independence dates, former names, capital moves and 303 dated changes to the map. Everyone gets the same eight questions, and a new logbook arrives at midnight, your time.

Stamps, the trivia wheel

Stamps is Mapelago's daily trivia wheel. Spin it and it lands on one of six categories. The six change every day: capitals, borders, languages, size, continents and coasts, with money, driving, peaks, phone codes and web addresses taking turns. Answer the geography question it gives you, and a right answer stamps that category. Miss, and the category gets one more chance with a new question. Miss again and it stays empty. The goal is to fill all six in as few spins as you can. Everyone gets the same questions each day, and the page clears at midnight, your time. When your page is done, Play more gives you endless practice questions that never change your page or streak.

Crossings, the border-route game

Crossings is Mapelago's daily route game. You get two countries joined by land, such as France and Poland, and you name the countries in between until your chain links them border to border. Par is the shortest route. Each country you name is coloured by how far it is from a shortest route, so the map warms up as you close in. Every guess beyond par costs 100 points, and a hint, which reveals a country on a shortest route, costs 150. Six guesses beyond par and the border stays shut. Only land borders count, so island countries can't be on a route. Borders that exist only through an exclave or overseas territory, such as Kaliningrad or French Guiana, aren't used in daily crossings. Monday's crossing is short and Sunday's is a long haul. Everyone gets the same crossing, and a new one arrives at midnight, your time.

Dead Reckoning, the blank map

Dead Reckoning names five places a day. Tap where each one is on a world map with no labels; pinch or scroll to zoom, or move the pin with the arrow keys and press Enter. Each place scores up to 100 by distance (a pin 250 km off still earns 80), and later places count for more, so a perfect day is 1,000. Your result is one square per place, from a bullseye to red, and never names the places. Play more gives endless practice that never touches your streak or stats.

Ballast, the load-the-ship game

Ballast is Mapelago's load-the-ship game. Real country outlines, all drawn at the same scale, come one at a time. Press and drag a country and it follows your finger; tap to turn it; let go and it drops from exactly there. The stack obeys gravity, and a country is lost only if it falls in the sea. To clear a level, the stack has to stand above the load line for two seconds. Daily Ballast is one run a day with the same cargo for everyone: level 1 is easy and every level after is harder, with more countries, a higher line, then swell, wind and long awkward shapes, in a new region each level. There's no top level. You have three lives; a load fails if a third country goes overboard or the cargo runs out below the line. After you place a country you can name it for bonus points, and your score is 100 for each level cleared plus 10 for each name. Endless practice is the same climb with a random cargo. On the thirty voyages you earn each country by answering a question about it first: a wrong answer costs nothing, but you get a plain crate instead.

The quick games: Bigger city? and Bigger country?

Bigger city? is Mapelago's one-minute population game. You see two cities and tap the one where more people live. Each reveal shows both populations, so a few answers may surprise you. There are ten pairs a day. They start far apart and get closer, so the last few are the hard ones. Everyone in the world gets the same ten pairs, and your result is a row of ten ticks and crosses. New pairs arrive at midnight, your time.

Bigger country? is Mapelago's one-minute size game. You see two countries and tap the one that covers more land. Sizes are total areas, using official figures where they differ from the map outlines, and countries whose size depends on what is counted are left out. Each reveal shows both countries at the same scale. There are ten pairs a day. They start far apart and get closer, so expect a close call or two at the end. Everyone in the world gets the same ten pairs, so you can compare notes with a friend. New pairs arrive at midnight, your time.

Good to know

  • Everyone in the world gets the same city and the same starting tiles, so you can compare results with friends.
  • A new city arrives at midnight, your time. Mondays are the easiest and Sundays the hardest.
  • If no tile can move, or your points reach zero, the board locks and you have to pin with the clues you have.
  • Your result is a row of colored squares with arrows. It never shows the city, so it's safe to post.
  • You can change how the game looks. Tap the half-filled circle at the top of the game and pick one of six looks. The rules and the scoring are the same in every one.
  • It's free. There's no account and nothing to install.

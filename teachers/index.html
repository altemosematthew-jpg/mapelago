Mapelago

Mapelago for teachers

Mapelago is ten short geography games that change every day. Everyone in the world gets the same puzzles, so a whole class can play together and compare answers at the end.

The short version

  • Free, with no accounts, no sign-up, no ads and no cookies.
  • Nothing to install. It runs in any up-to-date web browser, on a computer, a tablet or a phone.
  • Every student gets the same puzzles each day. New ones arrive at midnight, local time.
  • A copied result never contains the answer, so a student who shares a score doesn't spoil it for anyone.
  • Nothing about students is collected. Progress and streaks stay in the browser on that device, and the site counts page visits without cookies. Privacy has the details.

Which game for which lesson

For a five-minute warm-up, try Bigger country?, Bigger city? or Stamps. For a longer starter, try Mystery city, Crossings or Logbook.

GameHow longWhat it practises
Bigger country?Ten pairs, about a minuteScale: which of two countries covers more land, shown side by side at the same scale

FathomsSeven prompts, a few minutesBorders and capitals: naming a country that fits, such as one that borders Germany
Mystery cityAbout three minutesPlace: reading clues about hemisphere, continent, climate and language, then pinning the city on a world map
Mystery countryAbout the same as Mystery cityPlace: hemisphere, continent, coast and borders, languages, the capital's first letter and the country's outline
CrossingsLonger: a chain of countriesBorders: naming the countries between two others, border by border
LogbookEight questions, longerHistory: how the map has changed since 1800, from independence dates to former names and moved capitals
Dead ReckoningFive places, a few minutesPlace: finding cities and landmarks on a blank map, and judging distance and direction

Mystery city also has a Quick mode with no tiles: one clue to start, a new clue after each missed pin, and five pins. Mystery country gets the same on 27 November 2026.

How to run it

  • On a projector. Open today's game on the classroom computer and play one board together. The class calls the moves, talks through each clue and votes on where to drop the pin.
  • On student devices. Each student opens mapelago.com and plays the same puzzles. At the end they can copy their result to compare scores.
  • Without a connection. After the first visit to any Mapelago game, the browser keeps every game, so they work offline, which helps on patchy school wifi.
  • Support. Today's hints gives Mystery city's clues one at a time, without the answer.
